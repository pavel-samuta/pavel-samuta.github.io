OWNER-SIDE TECHNICAL REVIEW

The supplier has a proposal. The owner still needs an independent technical position.

A vendor proposal tells you what the vendor is prepared to supply. Before contract commitment, the owner needs to know what technical outcome is actually being purchased, what remains excluded and how compliance will be demonstrated.

What should be reviewed before the purchase order?

The technical specification, operating assumptions, interfaces, battery limits, exclusions, utilities, performance requirements, documentation package, spare-parts obligations and acceptance criteria.

Why compare technical offers before comparing price?

Two quotations can carry similar equipment names while including different scope, assumptions and performance obligations. A price comparison is weak until the technical differences are visible.

What is the deliverable?

A documented owner-side position: confirmed requirements, open questions, deviations, technical risks and the evidence required at the next decision gate.

What risks are easy to miss before signing?

Two suppliers can quote equipment with the same nominal capacity while offering different duty cycles, process tolerances, utilities, safety provisions, commissioning scope and documentation. Those differences become costly when the installation is already under way. A review maps requirements to evidence, identifies unanswered questions and makes exclusions visible before the commercial decision.

What should a technical review examine?

Depending on the equipment: interfaces with existing machines, drawings and layouts, material and component specifications, maintainability, critical spare parts, access for servicing, delivery limits, verification methods and responsibility for tests. Supplier claims are distinguished from tested performance and documented guarantees.

The output is a structured issue list and a proposed clarification or acceptance plan. It supports the owner's decision; it does not replace mandatory certification, independent inspection or contractual legal review.
