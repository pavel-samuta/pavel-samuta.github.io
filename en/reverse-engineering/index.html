INDUSTRIAL REVERSE ENGINEERING

From a physical part to manufacturing knowledge

Reverse engineering is useful when the part exists but the information needed to reproduce it does not. The deliverable should be more than a scan: it should give the owner a controlled basis for manufacture, inspection and future sourcing.

Engineering experience since 2007.

Can an obsolete part be reproduced without the original drawing?

Often yes, but confidence depends on the sample condition, function, mating components and what can be measured or independently established. Worn geometry must not automatically be treated as design geometry.

Why is measurement not the whole job?

Two parts can look geometrically identical and behave differently because of material, heat treatment, fits, surface finish or load path. Reverse engineering therefore has to recover engineering intent where the evidence allows it, not merely digitize surfaces.

What inputs are useful?

The physical sample, mating parts, photographs, old drawings or manuals, failure history, operating conditions, material records and any previous supplier documentation. Missing information is recorded as uncertainty rather than silently invented.

What can the output include?

3D CAD, manufacturing drawings, material and treatment requirements, critical interfaces, tolerances, inspection criteria and a package suitable for manufacturing enquiries.
